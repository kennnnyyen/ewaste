# E-recycling landing page plan

Prepared 13 October 2026. Status: planning brief. Use **[Business Name]** as the business-name placeholder. The page targets both scrap sellers and business data-destruction clients; operating details remain to be confirmed.

## 1. Recommended direction

Build a distinct brand for buying and recycling valuable computer components, with a clearly visible secure data-destruction service. Use one landing page with two enquiry paths: **Sell computer scrap** and **Arrange data destruction**.

Working assumptions:

- “PCGs” means PCBs (printed circuit boards).
- The business buys selected components as well as providing recycling services; confirm this commercial model before final copy.
- Scrap sellers and business data-destruction customers are equally important audiences, as confirmed by the user. Give both a clear path from the hero.
- The new business is a sister company of YP ECYCLE, as described in the brief. The exact legal and operating relationship needs to be supplied.
- Service area, premises, collection, shipping, minimum quantities, payment terms, and turnaround times are not yet established.

The main conversion is a qualified enquiry. Measure scrap enquiries and data-destruction enquiries separately so the business can see which service attracts useful leads.

## 2. Reference findings

[The Board Guy](https://www.theboardguy.com.au/) explicitly explains which components it buys, offers a prices link, and describes drop-off and mail-in options. Take inspiration from that clarity: identify materials visually, explain the transaction, and make requesting a quote straightforward. Its prices and operating promises belong to that business and should not be transferred to the new site.

[YP ECYCLE](https://ypecycle.com.au/) introduces IT asset recovery, recycling, and data destruction, and lists ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 45001, and AS/NZS 5377. Its [certifications page](https://ypecycle.com.au/certifications) provides document links. Use the relationship as attributed evidence, with accurate wording about which company performs the work and holds each certificate.

These findings are based on published page content. Certificate documents, validity, legal entity coverage, and permission to reuse certification marks have not yet been verified.

## 3. Positioning and draft copy

Brand character: knowledgeable, practical, secure, and approachable. Use Australian English and specific service language.

Provisional hero headline:

> Computer scrap recycling. Secure data destruction.

Provisional supporting copy:

> Recover value from your PCBs, CPUs and selected computer components with [Business Name], and enquire about secure data destruction for retired IT equipment.

Use two clearly visible actions: **Get a scrap quote** and **Enquire about data destruction**. Present them side by side on desktop and stacked on mobile, with comparable prominence and brief audience-specific context. Both lead to the same form with the relevant service selected. A visible phone link supports customers who prefer to talk.

Once location and coverage are confirmed, include the actual service area close to these actions. Use “A sister company of YP ECYCLE” as a small supporting line after the relationship wording is confirmed.

Avoid repeating the same certification badges throughout the page. The first appearance provides quick reassurance; the detailed section provides evidence.

Do not publish fixed scrap prices unless the business can maintain them. For launch, use assessed quotes with an explanation that value depends on category, grade, quantity, and current buying rates. If published prices are later introduced, include units, an update date, and the applicable terms.

## 5. Visual design and new logo

Recommended direction: graphite, off-white, deep green, and a restrained copper accent. This connects electronics, material recovery, and trust without overwhelming the content.

Initial palette, to be contrast-tested during design:

- Graphite: `#172321` for headings and dark sections.
- Off-white: `#F6F7F2` for the main background.
- Deep green: `#176B50` for primary actions.
- Copper: `#B56D3C` for decorative details.
- White: `#FFFFFF` for cards and inverse text.

Use a readable sans-serif typeface, generous spacing, clear headings, and concise text. The desktop hero pairs copy with a close-up photograph of actual components. Mobile places the message and actions first, followed by imagery. Use a subtle circuit pattern sparingly and small transitions that respect reduced-motion preferences.

Prioritise original photography of sorted components, the team, and approved operating areas. Obtain usage rights for all third-party imagery. Genuine facility and equipment photographs should support operational claims.

Logo brief:

- Explore three concepts: a circuit trace forming a recovery loop, a chip outline with a circular path, and a name-based monogram once the business name is known.
- Recommended starting point: the circuit recovery loop with a simple wordmark; it directly communicates component recovery and works across services.
- Keep the symbol legible at favicon size and in one colour.
- Deliver editable SVG masters, horizontal and stacked versions, symbol-only favicon, light/dark versions, transparent PNG exports, and a short usage guide.
- Give the new business its own logo. Keep the YP ECYCLE endorsement secondary and use its existing logo only with permission.

## 6. Certification and data-destruction content

Reference all four standards listed by YP ECYCLE: ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 45001, and AS/NZS 5377. Match the exact designation and edition to the certificate documents before publication.

Provisional attribution:

> A sister company of YP ECYCLE. View YP ECYCLE's certifications.

Only describe the new business as certified where current documentation covers its legal entity, location, and activities. If YP ECYCLE performs a service, say so explicitly once the operating arrangement is confirmed. Publish approved certification marks rather than inventing badges or using the ISO organisation logo as a certification mark.

Explain data destruction through evidence customers can assess: supported media, method, collection and custody, tracking where available, onsite/offsite options, and the exact report or certificate issued. Confirm these details with the operator. Do not assume degaussing applies to SSDs or flash memory; method descriptions must distinguish media types.

Use precise, supported wording for sanitisation standards. Claims such as “military grade”, “NSA approved”, “100% landfill diversion”, or absolute recovery guarantees need supporting evidence specific to the service being advertised.

## 7. Enquiry experience

Use an accessible service selector: **Sell computer scrap**, **Data destruction**, or **Both**. The CTA sets the initial selection; users can change it.

Shared fields: name, email, optional phone, optional business name, postcode, and a short description. Require at least one reliable contact method; for the simplest launch form, require email. Ask for material category and approximate weight/quantity for scrap enquiries, and media type/device count plus preferred service location for destruction enquiries. Include “Not sure” options.

Optional component photographs improve initial grading. If included at launch, allow a small number of JPEG/PNG/WebP images with explicit size limits, private object storage, short-lived access, validation, and an agreed retention period. If the team does not need this workflow immediately, request photos during follow-up and keep launch scope smaller.

Show inline validation, a pending state, clear confirmation, and an actionable failure state that preserves entered values. Confirm receipt only after the server successfully hands the enquiry to the delivery service. A timeout must not appear as a successful submission. Use idempotent submission handling to reduce duplicates during retries.

Link the privacy notice beside submission. Keep optional marketing consent separate. Record only essential enquiry details and avoid logging contact information or photographs. Analytics should record service category and event name, without form contents.

## 8. Technology and architecture

Use current stable versions at implementation time, pin resolved versions in the lockfile, and review available security patches before deployment.

| Layer | Recommendation | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framework | Latest stable Next.js, App Router; currently 16.4 | Modern routing, server rendering, metadata, and native Vercel deployment. |
| UI | Compatible React release; Next.js 16.4 includes React 19.3 | Follow the framework's supported combination. |
| Language | TypeScript with strict checking | Clear content models and validated integration boundaries. |
| Styling | Tailwind CSS, current stable 4.x; documentation currently shows 4.3 | Responsive layouts and consistent design tokens. |
| Runtime | Node.js 24 LTS on Vercel | Vercel currently supports and defaults to 24.x. |
| Content | Typed local content files | Simple maintenance for a small landing page. |
| Enquiries | Next.js Route Handler and a transactional email provider | Keep validation and credentials on the server. |
| Optional uploads | Managed private object storage | Persistent storage independent of serverless filesystem lifecycle. |
| Hosting | Vercel connected to the Git repository | Preview deployments, production deployment, and rollback. |

Sources: [Next.js 16.4 release](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16-4), [Tailwind Next.js guide](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/installation/framework-guides/nextjs), [Vercel Node.js versions](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes/node-js/node-js-versions), and [Next.js on Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/frameworks/full-stack/nextjs).

Keep marketing sections rendered on the server and prerender/cache public content according to the installed Next.js version. Limit client JavaScript to the mobile navigation and interactive form. Use Next.js image and font optimisation. Prefer native semantic controls and add a component library only where complex interactions justify it.

Separate page presentation, content data, enquiry validation, and email delivery. Keep the provider adapter replaceable without creating a general-purpose integration framework. Define delivery timeouts, safe errors, and minimal failure logging. Protect the form with server-side validation, payload limits, bot checks, and shared rate limiting or provider-managed abuse protection; process-local counters are unreliable across serverless instances.

Launch routes: `/`, `/privacy`, and a small `/certifications` route if certificate details become too long for the landing page. No customer account, CMS, custom database, or pricing engine is necessary for the initial scope. Add them later only for demonstrated editorial or operational needs.

## 9. SEO, accessibility, and verification

Write one descriptive H1, useful section headings, title and description, canonical URL, social preview metadata, sitemap, and robots rules. Add structured business/service data containing only verified details. Target component and data-destruction search terms for the confirmed location; do not imply nationwide coverage without an established service.

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard navigation, visible focus, labels, error announcements, adequate contrast, readable type, accessible menus, and reduced-motion support. Avoid a mobile sticky action that covers content or the form.

Performance targets for real-user measurement: LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1. Lab checks guide optimisation; they do not prove field performance before traffic exists.

Before launch, run type checking, linting, and a production build. Add focused tests for enquiry validation, provider failures/timeouts, duplicate handling, and upload restrictions if used. Browser checks cover both enquiry paths, keyboard use, mobile layout, navigation, and visible success/failure states. Verify actual inbox delivery, HTTPS, canonical domain, metadata, certificate links, and preview indexing rules.

## 10. Delivery sequence and estimate

Indicative engineering/design estimate: **8–12 working days**, assuming timely business information, one consolidated design revision, and available photography. Optional upload handling and additional revision rounds may increase the scope.

1. Business/content confirmation, relationship wording, and evidence collection: 1–2 days.
2. Logo exploration, desktop/mobile wireframes, and visual direction: 2–3 days.
3. Next.js implementation, content integration, and enquiry delivery: 3–4 days.
4. Accessibility, functional verification, optimisation, and Vercel launch preparation: 2–3 days.

Deliverables: logo package, responsive page design, production source, functioning enquiry flow, verified business content, preview deployment, and deployment/maintenance notes. Finalise launch details in the business's Vercel account, verify domain and email ownership, and document who responds to enquiries and maintains certificates.
